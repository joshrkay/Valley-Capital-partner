When a lender sells a hotel loan, the owner usually learns of it from a letter. The letter is the start of the conversation, not the end of it. These five points are worth having in writing before anything changes.

## 1. Who holds the loan now

The name and address of the new holder, and where notices should go. The [assignment of the mortgage](/glossary/#assignment-of-mortgage) is usually recorded in the local land records, so it can be checked there.

## 2. Who services it

The holder and the servicer can be different firms. Ask who receives payments, how, and from which date. If payment instructions change, confirm them through a contact you already know, such as the number on earlier loan statements, before sending money. Do not rely on a single new email.

## 3. What is owed

A written statement, as of a stated date, of principal, interest, fees, and any [protective advances](/glossary/#protective-advance) the lender has made.

## 4. Which earlier agreements still stand

A [forbearance](/glossary/#forbearance), a [modification](/glossary/#loan-modification), or an extension letter. The loan documents pass to the new holder as they are, so these should carry over. Ask the new holder to say so in writing, so that there is no doubt later.

## 5. Who to talk to

The person with authority to agree to terms, and how proposals should be made. A conversation with someone who cannot decide costs time that a hotel in default may not have.

## If the answers do not come

Keep meeting the payment obligations in the loan documents, to the party you have confirmed, and tell your counsel. The earlier post on [what changes, and what does not, when your hotel loan is sold](/blog/when-your-hotel-loan-is-sold/) covers what to expect and what an owner can prepare.

## What we don't know

Whether and how a lender must notify a borrower of a sale depends on the loan documents and on state law. Reading yours is the first step.
