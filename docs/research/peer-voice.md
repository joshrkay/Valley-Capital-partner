# How peers write about hotel loan distress

Research date: November 12, 2026. Window: roughly November 2025 to November 2026. Question: how do other organizations that cover or work in US hotel loan distress talk about it, and what does that mean for our voice, topics and news triggers?

## Bottom line

1. **There are two dominant registers and neither is ours.** Research houses and law firms write impersonal, hedged, authority-citing notes. Brokers write transaction announcements. Nobody writes as a hotel-note buyer with a point of view, and no special servicer publishes its own workout commentary. (Observed in the searches; the second half is absence of evidence.)
2. **The same gaps show up in all three groups.** What a note buyer does after closing, what a hotel note trades for as a percentage of balance, franchise and license consequences of a note sale, receiver mechanics and state timelines, closing certainty, and non-CMBS bank loans. These are the topics where a plain buyer-side explainer is missing. (Inference.)
3. **Headline numbers conflict, and nobody reconciles them.** Trepp, KBRA and CRED iQ use different definitions. The first rule for any news post is to state the measure, the source and the date in one sentence.
4. **Press names people; research houses name deals and brands; brokers anonymize but leak through URLs.** Our "pattern, not party" rule is stricter than the press and about the same as the research houses.
5. **We found no evidence either way on whether a calm tone performs better.** No engagement data was reachable, and no aggressive voice was seen visibly thriving. The case for calm rests on audience and legal risk, not on proof.

## Method and limits (read this first)

About 125 web searches across three groups of peers (capital and transaction side, legal and process side, data and press side). **No peer page was read.** Every fetch failed: DNS errors from the fetch tool and HTTP 403 on CONNECT from the session proxy for roughly 35 to 55 hosts per agent, including connectcre.com, credaily.com, bisnow.com, therealdeal.com, kbra.com, hvs.com, nge.com, buchalter.com and crefc.org. The agents did not try to get around the block.

So every fact below comes from search-result excerpts, which are paraphrases and sometimes conflict. Specifically **not established**: sentence length, jargon level, how long a "Return to Lender" issue is, the body text of any article, the real disclaimer wording on most law-firm blogs, and whether any trigger URL loads. Dates come from titles, URLs or excerpts; "undated" means none was visible. Trepp and Fitch could not be searched at all. Case-caption headlines from Buchalter are partly reconstructed from URL slugs.

The raw notes from each agent (with fuller citations) were written to the session scratchpad and are not kept in the repo.

## Observed

### Who publishes

- **Capital and transaction side:** HVS (hvs.com/publications; US Market Pulse, conference takeaways), Hodges Ward Elliott (per-deal microsites and releases), Hilco Real Estate (auction releases), JLL Hotels & Hospitality (financing releases, an outlook, a live listing for a $90 million non-performing Beverly Hills loan), Newmark, Eastdil Secured, CBRE, Cushman & Wakefield, Marcus & Millichap (October 2026 hotel outlook), Colliers, Matthews (insights and quarterly auction reports), KBRA (monthly CMBS trends and a June 2026 lodging study), Mission Capital Advisors (hotel content may be old), Avana Capital (a hotel lender). No hotel-note buyer's own publishing was found; Blackstone, Yellowstone, Kalthia and 109Co appear only in press. Source: Ring A notes, searches on November 12, 2026.
- **Legal and process side:** Buchalter's "Franchise Frontlines" is the most active hotel-specific publisher (hotel posts dated October 24, 2025 to May 8, 2026, https://www.buchalter.com/blogs/). Others: Neal, Gerber & Eisenberg, Katten Quickreads, Mayer Brown, FBT Gibbons ("CREF Roundup" weekly), Arnold & Porter, Lathrop GPM, McDonald Hopkins, Schlam Stone & Dolan, JMBM's Hotel Law Blog (nothing from 2026 surfaced), receivers and operators (MCA Financial, HVS Hotel Management, Aimbridge Prism Lender Services, Concord, Highgate), Bondoro (filing alerts), CREFC and SitusAMC (data and webinars). Nothing hotel-workout-specific from ABI, AHLA or MBA in the window. Special servicers (Rialto, K-Star, Midland, LNR) publish nothing of their own that was found.
- **Data and press side:** Connect CRE ("Return to Lender", weekly on Thursdays, latest edition November 12, 2026: https://www.connectcre.com/stories/return-to-lender-week-of-nov-12-2026/), CRE Daily, Bisnow, The Real Deal, Commercial Observer, GlobeSt, CoStar, Skift, Hospitality Investor, Hospitality Net, plus data from Trepp, KBRA and CRED iQ.

### What they are saying

- **Delinquency is lumpy and driven by single loans.** Trepp lodging delinquency: 7.31% in March, 5.22% in June, 6.18% in September, 5.94% in October (CRE Daily, https://www.credaily.com/?p=219707; Connect CRE, https://www.connectcre.com/?p=504241).
- **Hilton-branded hotels are over-represented.** KBRA, June 3, 2026: Hilton $2.5 billion delinquent, 15.7%, lodging overall 6.4% as of March (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260603616364/en/KBRA-Releases-Research-Late-Checkout-Lodging-Delinquencies-Vary-by-Price-Class-and-Chain). The Real Deal relayed it on June 22 (https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/06/22/hilton-branded-hotels-account-for-outsized-share-of-distress/). On Trepp's different "nonperforming" measure, Hilton is 11.73% against Marriott's 7.23% (https://www.credaily.com/?p=223465, undated).
- **Which segment is worst is contested.** KBRA says upscale and upper-upscale lead by balance; Trepp-based CRE Daily pieces say limited-service leads (https://www.credaily.com/?p=218494 and https://www.credaily.com/?p=223388, both undated).
- **The maturity wall is agreed on size and disputed on danger.** The MBA figure is about 30% of hotel and motel loans due in 2026 (via Bisnow, https://www.bisnow.com/news/los-angeles/hotel/california-hotels-distress-sales-first-half-2026, August 17, 2026). HVS on February 12, 2026 calls distress "Emerging But Not Yet Systemic" (https://www.hvs.com/article/10378-MBA-CREF-2026-Conference-Key-Takeaways-Debt-Markets-Liquidity-and-the-Evolving-Real-Estate-Cycle); on March 2 it expects a "meaningful uptick" in distressed deals (https://www.hvs.com/article/10392-what-every-owner-needs-to-know-before-deciding-to-sell--hold--or-renovate-in-2026). Neal, Gerber & Eisenberg, May 26, 2026, says "extend and pretend" is ending (https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/the-end-of-extend-and-pretend-for-hotel-1927223/).
- **Legal friction is the live process story.** Receiver limits (*Kolessar*, Alabama Supreme Court, October 24, 2025, https://www.buchalter.com/blogs/kolessar-v-sjp-investment-partners-llc-alabama-supreme-court-reverses-order-requiring-receiver-to-pay-pre-receivership-hotel-debts/; one summary calls it unpublished), bankruptcy used to avoid a receiver (*Chicago South Loop Hotel Owner*, March 10, 2026, https://www.ilnb.uscourts.gov/sites/ilnb/files/opinions/ChicagoSouthLoopHotel2512829.pdf), and franchise agreements kept alive in Chapter 11 despite missed improvement-plan deadlines (*Welcome Group 2*, November 19, 2025, https://www.buchalter.com/blogs/in-re-welcome-group-2-llc-bankruptcy-court-permits-hotel-franchisee-to-assume-hilton-agreement-despite-missed-pip-deadlines).
- **A note buyer's paperwork risk is in the news today.** A foreclosure by a note buyer stalled when the judge found the note papers not properly attached (The Real Deal, November 12, 2026, https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/11/12/morris-moinian-foreclosure-stalled-in-staple-case/).
- **Buying debt to get the property is a recognized route.** Blackstone reportedly held the loan and won Stanly Ranch at a $195 million foreclosure sale on March 27, 2026 (Bloomberg, https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-30/blackstone-acquires-napa-valley-resort-in-a-foreclosure-sale). Neal, Gerber & Eisenberg tells readers to "Consider buying the debt."
- **Pricing evidence is anecdotal.** California, first half of 2026: 28% of sales and 37% of dollar volume involved lender action (Atlas data via Bisnow, August 17, 2026, link above). A Long Beach hotel sold for $42 million against $85 million nine years earlier (same article).
- **Lodging special-servicing rates differ by publisher:** 8.89% in June 2026 (CREFC, report released July 28, https://www.crefc.org/cre/content/News/Items/Research_and_Data/2026/CREFCs_June_2026_Monthly_CMBS_Loan_Performance_Report.aspx) and 8.74% in August (Trepp via one summary, single source).

### Why the headline numbers differ

Trepp lodging delinquency (about 6%, share of CMBS balance, monthly), KBRA's 6.4% (rated deals only, March), and CRED iQ's "distress" (about 12%, a broader definition and a metro average, https://www.credaily.com/?p=221842) measure different things. Trepp "nonperforming" against KBRA "delinquent" explains Hilton at 11.7% against 15.7%. No source found reconciles them in one place.

### How they write

- **Legal blogs:** impersonal past-tense holdings, statutes and docket numbers cited, parties named from court records, "may weigh differently" hedging. Headline form is case caption, colon, verb-led holding. The exception is Neal, Gerber & Eisenberg: second person, directive ("It is not all dollars and cents"), addressed to "owners, operators, and opportunistic purchasers."
- **Data houses:** quantitative and un-moralized. KBRA uses a pun headline ("Late Checkout") over tables to one decimal. CREFC is institutional and passive.
- **Press:** CRE Daily headlines carry a number and a verb ("CMBS Delinquency Rate Dips to 7.87% as Retail Jumps 63 bps", https://www.credaily.com/?p=224934, November 2026). Bisnow is plain-spoken and records outreach ("didn't immediately respond"). The Real Deal is wry and litigation-anchored and names individuals in headlines. Connect CRE gives each loan an inline tag, for example "($745.7 million | THPT 2023-THL)", credits local business journals and Morningstar Credit by name, and shows paper-against-cash prices.
- **Brokers:** "[Firm] Arranges/Announces/Brokers [size] [asset]", formal third person, "exclusively retained."
- **Headline samples:** "In re Welcome Group 2: Bankruptcy Court Permits Hotel Franchisee to Assume Hilton Agreement Despite Missed PIP Deadlines" (Buchalter, November 19, 2025); "Marriott Hotel in Receivership After $45 Million Default" (The Real Deal, June 1, 2026, https://therealdeal.com/la/2026/06/01/marriott-hotel-in-receivership-after-45-million-default/); "When Hotel Financing Fails, Know Who Holds Which Cards" (Commercial Observer, November 11, 2026, https://commercialobserver.com/2026/11/hotel-financing-distress-stakeholders-resolutions/); "Takeaways From ALIS Law 2026: Where Hotel Deals Actually Get Stuck and How to Keep Them Moving" (Katten, February 2026, https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/hotels-hospitality/1744228/takeaways-from-alis-law-2026-where-hotel-deals-actually-get-stuck-and-how-to-keep-them-moving).

### Names, hedges and disclaimers

- The Real Deal, Commercial Observer and Connect CRE name sponsors and principals, often in the headline, with "allegedly" or "alleging" and filing-based attribution. Data firms and CRE Daily name loans and brands, not owners. Servicer remarks are usually attributed to Morningstar Credit.
- Brokers hide names in the text and leak them in URLs: the JLL listing says "the Seller" and puts Beverly Hills in its URL; a Hodges Ward Elliott microsite puts the town in its subdomain (https://lakeplacidloansale3q25.hodgeswardelliott.com).
- No corrections or retractions were found. Risk shows instead as inconsistent facts across outlets, for example the same hotel loan's maturity given as 2027 in one outlet and September 2028 in another, and a Bisnow URL reading "557m" under a $577 million headline.
- Confirmed disclaimers: Buchalter's site-wide notice (informational, not legal advice, no attorney-client relationship, https://www.buchalter.com/disclaimer/) and Katten's event-page wording. For most others no disclaimer text was visible.
- Promotional "path to ownership" framing: a 2024 note-sale release was titled "Rare Opportunity to Secure Ownership" (Business Wire, June 3, 2024, outside the window). Allegations of predatory loan-to-own conduct against lenders appear in The Real Deal (June 25, 2024, https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2024/06/25/how-a-bold-staten-island-hotel-project-went-bankrupt; January 5, 2023, https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2023/01/05/inside-maverick-real-estates-bag-of-tricks/). These are allegations, not findings.

### Tone and counter-evidence

Promotional voice is common among brokers, receivers (Aimbridge claims 300+ receiver appointments, https://aimbridgehospitality.com/prismlenderservices) and JMBM. Matthews uses water metaphors ("The Rising Tide of Hotel Delinquency", https://www.matthews.com/insights/hotel-delinquency, undated) and calls renovation-burdened midscale hotels a "buying opportunity" (https://www.matthews.com/insights/a-reality-check-for-us-hotel-investors, undated). Alarm headlines exist ("Report: Hotel Industry Faces Historic Wave of Foreclosures", https://lodgingmagazine.com/report-hotel-industry-faces-historic-wave-of-foreclosures-as-delinquencies-rise/, undated). **No engagement data was available**, and no promotional or sensational voice was seen visibly thriving. The most energetic credible voice, The Real Deal, takes its energy from court filings.

## Inferences

- The lane no peer occupies is a plain, specific, buyer-side explainer on process, pricing evidence and what happens after a note sale. Part of the reason may be that loan-to-own framing carries legal and reputational risk, which would explain why active buyers publish nothing.
- The promotional pieces are written to attract bidders. Their audience is different from bank special-assets staff, so their tone says little about what those readers want.
- Calm and precise fits the research and legal peers and avoids the allegations that attach to ownership-seeking framing. This is a judgment about audience and risk, not a finding from performance data.

## Recommendations

**Voice** (applied to `docs/voice.md`):
- State the measure, source and as-of date in one sentence. Never set one publisher's rate beside another's without saying the definitions differ.
- Date every post and add a "last checked" line when it relies on figures or a ruling. For a ruling, give the court, date, whether it is published, whether it is state-specific, and what it does not decide.
- Attribute every claim about a party ("according to a court filing dated ...", "alleges"). Check the plaintiff of record against the originating lender.
- Check URLs, file names and image names for identities the text leaves out.
- No puns, no weather or water images, no "path to ownership" or "opportunity" framing.

**Topics to cover first** (gaps found in all three groups): what a note buyer does after closing; how a note sale interacts with a receivership; franchise, improvement-plan and license consequences of a note sale; what a purchased note's paper trail must show (the stalled-foreclosure story is the live example); reconciling Trepp, KBRA and CRED iQ; state-by-state timelines; non-CMBS bank-held loans.

**News desk listeners:** use the trigger table above as the first source list. Adopt the Connect CRE habit of crediting the original filing or local journal by name.

**Infrastructure consequence:** the proxy blocked nearly every peer and press host from this cloud container. A daily scheduled task running inside this environment would hit the same wall. A GitHub Actions job runs on open internet and would not.
