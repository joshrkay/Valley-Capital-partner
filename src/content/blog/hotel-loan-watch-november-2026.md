Hotel loan delinquency in commercial mortgage-backed securities eased in October, and hotel results kept rising year over year. The latest lodging special servicing readings found are from the summer. All of these readings describe securitized loans, not hotel loans held by banks.

## The numbers

- **Trepp, lodging delinquency, October 2026: 5.94%.** That is 24 basis points below September's 6.18% ([Connect CRE](https://www.connectcre.com/?p=504241)). Earlier readings were 5.22% in June ([CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/?p=219707)) and 5.84% in August ([CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/briefs/cmbs-delinquency-holds-at-7-85-as-office-risk-rises/)).
- **Trepp, all property types, October 2026: 7.87%,** down from 8.02% in September. The fall came mainly from a $975 million showroom loan returning to current. Retail rose 63 basis points ([CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/?p=224934)).
- **KBRA, 30-plus-day delinquency, rated private-label CMBS, all property types, October 2026: 7.7%,** down 2 basis points from September ([KBRA](https://www.kbra.com/publications/qYPWnQCj)). KBRA's universe and method differ from Trepp's, so the two are not comparable. KBRA's latest lodging figure found is 6.4% as of March ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260603616364/en/KBRA-Releases-Research-Late-Checkout-Lodging-Delinquencies-Vary-by-Price-Class-and-Chain)).
- **CREFC, lodging special servicing, June 2026: 8.89%,** up 44 basis points on large new transfers ([CREFC](https://www.crefc.org/cre/content/News/Items/Research_and_Data/2026/CREFCs_June_2026_Monthly_CMBS_Loan_Performance_Report.aspx)). No later lodging reading was found. Across all property types, Trepp put special servicing at 11.42% in August, the highest since February 2013 ([CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/?p=222831)).
- **CoStar weekly data, October 2026:** [revenue per available room](/glossary/#revpar) was up year over year in each of the three weeks found: 5.5% for October 11 to 17 ([Hotel Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/us-hotel-performance-gains-momentum-as-revpar-rises-55)), 12.6% for October 18 to 24 ([Lodging](https://lodgingmagazine.com/costar-u-s-hotel-industry-reports-positive-year-over-year-comparisons-oct-29/)), and 9.7% for October 25 to 31 ([Hotel Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/us-hotel-performance-strengthens-in-late-october-as-revpar-jumps-nearly-10)). A single week can swing on local events, so read these as direction, not a monthly total. HVS's October market pulse put occupancy about two points below 2019 levels, with the gains coming from rate ([Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/report/4135017/hvs-us-market-pulse-october-2026)).

## What moved

Lodging delinquency has run 5.22%, 5.84%, 6.18%, and 5.94% in June, August, September, and October. It rose into September and gave back part of that rise in October. No July reading was found.

The overall rate fell mainly because one large loan, a showroom loan, returned to current, which says little about hotels. A single loan can move a rate weighted by balance.

A loan that is extended or modified can leave the delinquent count without having been repaid. A lower rate does not by itself show that the underlying loans have been resolved.

## How to read these numbers

**What each counts.** A delinquency rate counts loans that have missed a payment by a stated number of days; KBRA's reading here uses 30 or more. A special servicing rate counts loans a [special servicer](/glossary/#special-servicer) has taken over, some still current, so it can rise first. Only the lodging lines speak to hotels.

**Why the publishers differ.** They draw on different loans and define the measure in their own ways, and the lodging figures here are from different months: KBRA's is as of March, Trepp's is for October. Close readings would not show agreement, and distant ones would not show error.

**Weekly hotel results.** They show direction, and little else. Revenue per available room is revenue; a loan is repaid from the income left after costs. Loans come under stress hotel by hotel, when income cannot cover the payment or the balance cannot be refinanced.

## What this means for a lender

For a lender whose hotel loans sit in none of these pools, the numbers are context. The useful comparison is the share of its own hotel loans [past due](/glossary/#past-due) by 30 days or more, by balance and by count, against a publisher's figure for the same month.

For each loan, a few questions beat any market rate. Is it current on its original terms or on a [modification](/glossary/#loan-modification)? When does it mature, and could the hotel's income support a refinance? How does it compare with its [competitive set](/glossary/#comp-set)?

A lodging reading that moves with no single large loan to explain it, or special servicing rising while delinquency stays low, would change the picture. Neither calls for action by itself. Each is a reason to read the lender's own measures first.

## What is due next

- **Trepp's November reading:** typically in the first week of the following month, so early December.
- **KBRA's November CMBS report:** KBRA publishes near month-end. Its October 2025 edition is dated October 31, 2025 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251031928676/en/KBRA-Releases-Research-CMBS-Loan-Performance-Trends-October-2025)).
- **FDIC Quarterly Banking Profile, third quarter:** late November, inferred from the second-quarter release in late August. It covers loans banks hold on their own books.

## What we don't know

These rates cover securitized loans only and say nothing about hotel loans held by banks. We have not compared Trepp's and KBRA's definitions line by line, and we did not find lodging special servicing for July through October. We do not know what drove October's lodging decline, and we have not checked whether the FDIC release separates hotel loans.
