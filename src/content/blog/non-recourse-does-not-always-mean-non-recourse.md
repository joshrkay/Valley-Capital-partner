Owners often describe a hotel loan as non-recourse. They mean that if the loan is not repaid, the lender can take the hotel and nothing else. That is the starting position in most commercial loans. It is not always where a loan ends up.

## What non-recourse usually means

A non-recourse loan limits the lender to its collateral, which is the hotel and the assets that go with it. The owners are not personally liable for the debt itself.

## Where carve-outs come in

Most non-recourse loans come with a separate promise from the owner's principals, called a [non-recourse carve-out](/glossary/#non-recourse-carve-out) guaranty, and sometimes a "bad boy" guaranty. It lists the acts that remove the protection. The list differs from loan to loan. Common entries are:

- Fraud or misrepresentation to the lender.
- Taking rents, insurance proceeds, or other money that belonged to the property.
- Transferring the hotel, or ownership of the borrower, without the lender's consent.
- Letting taxes, liens, or insurance go unpaid.
- A voluntary bankruptcy filing, or one the owners help bring about.

In some loans the list also reaches the franchise, so losing the flag can be one of the triggers.

The wording decides how large the exposure is. Some carve-outs make the guarantor liable only for the losses the lender suffers because of the act. Others make the guarantor liable for the whole debt.

## Why hotels raise the stakes

A hotel handles cash every day, collects occupancy and sales taxes for the government, depends on a franchise it can lose, and usually has a manager who controls the bank accounts. That gives it more ways to trip a carve-out than a building with a handful of leases.

## What a loan sale does

A [note sale](/glossary/#note-sale) does not rewrite the guaranty. It passes to the new holder with the rest of the loan documents, as [what changes, and what does not, when your hotel loan is sold](/blog/when-your-hotel-loan-is-sold/) explains. What can change is the holder's willingness to agree to a release or a limit. A release of the [guaranty](/glossary/#guaranty) is often part of a negotiated transfer of the hotel.

## What to check

- Read the guaranty itself, not the summary of terms.
- For each listed act, find out whether it makes the guarantor liable for losses or for the full debt.
- Note which triggers depend on a choice the borrower makes, and which can happen without one.
- Before taking any step in a default that a carve-out might reach, such as a transfer, a filing, or moving cash between accounts, speak to your own counsel.

## What we don't know

These documents vary by lender, by securitization, and by state. Whether a particular act triggers liability is a legal question about particular words, and nothing here replaces counsel who has read the guaranty.
