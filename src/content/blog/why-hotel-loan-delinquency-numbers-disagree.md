Published figures for hotel loan stress in 2026 run from about 6% for lodging as a whole to about 16% for one brand's loans. The publishers are not contradicting each other. They count different loans, by different rules, on different dates.

A market rate describes one pool of securitized hotel loans, under one publisher's definition, in one month. It cannot say how a lender's own hotel loans are doing.

## Three readings of the same market

- **Trepp, lodging delinquency, October 2026: 5.94%.** Trepp reports monthly on hotel loans in commercial mortgage-backed securities. The October reading was 24 basis points below September's ([Connect CRE](https://www.connectcre.com/?p=504241)).
- **KBRA, lodging delinquency as of March 2026: 6.4%.** KBRA's study, released June 3, covers the deals it rates and splits the figure by hotel chain and price class ([KBRA, via Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260603616364/en/KBRA-Releases-Research-Late-Checkout-Lodging-Delinquencies-Vary-by-Price-Class-and-Chain)).
- **The same brand, two answers.** KBRA put Hilton-branded hotels at 15.7% delinquent. Trepp put Hilton at 11.73% on its "nonperforming" measure, against 7.23% for Marriott ([CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/?p=223465)).

CRED iQ publishes its own monthly distress rate, which also counts loans in special servicing, so it runs higher than a pure delinquency rate. The two Hilton figures are about four points apart, and the "nonperforming" one is lower, so a label alone does not predict a gap's direction.

## Why the figures differ

**What counts as trouble.** Each publisher sets its own rule, and its methodology note governs. Three tests are common.

**Late on payments** counts a loan once a payment is missed by a stated number of days. It misses a loan that is current until maturity and then cannot repay ([maturity default](/glossary/#maturity-default)), and a loan that was extended or modified and is current on its new terms.

**Nonperforming** sets a different bar. In bank usage a loan is commonly [non-performing](/glossary/#non-performing-loan) when it is on non-accrual or 90 days or more past due, so a loan one month late can be delinquent without being nonperforming.

**Special-serviced** counts loans a [special servicer](/glossary/#special-servicer) has taken over at default or at imminent risk of default. It catches trouble early, because some of those loans are still current.

**Which loans are in the pool.** All securitized hotel loans, only the deals one agency rates, or one brand or city. KBRA's study title says delinquencies vary by price class and chain, so the mix matters.

**Weighting.** A rate weighted by balance lets one large loan dominate. A rate weighted by number of loans counts each once. Trepp's lodging rate fell 79 basis points to 5.22% in June 2026, a drop reported as the result of a large Florida portfolio loan returning to current ([CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/?p=219707)).

**The date.** A monthly reading, a quarter-end figure, and a study published months later do not line up. KBRA's lodging figure is as of March and Trepp's is for October.

## Same hotels, four rates

**Illustration (invented numbers):** A pool holds ten hotel loans totaling $100 million. One loan, $5 million, is 45 days late and has gone to the special servicer. Another, $30 million, is current but was transferred because its maturity is close. Late loans are 10% of the pool by number and 5% by balance. Special-serviced loans are 20% by number and 35% by balance.

None of the four is wrong. Each answers a different question: how many loans have missed a payment, or how much money sits with a servicer.

## What a lender can use instead

These rates describe securitized loans. They do not cover hotel loans held on a bank's own balance sheet, which is where many community-bank hotel loans sit. A bank's own records answer its question better: [past due](/glossary/#past-due) status, [non-accrual](/glossary/#non-accrual) loans, [classified loans](/glossary/#classified-loan), and the [watch list](/glossary/#watch-list).

Add what no delinquency rate shows: maturities coming over the next year, and loans that are current only because they were extended or modified. To compare with a market rate, restate the lender's figure on the publisher's definition and say that the pools differ. The result is context, not a benchmark.

## Mistakes when quoting a market rate

- **Dropping the label.** Quote the measure, publisher, and month together: "Trepp's lodging delinquency rate was 5.94% in October."
- **Comparing publishers.** KBRA's 6.4% for March set against Trepp's 5.94% for October is not a fall. The measure, pool, and date all differ.
- **Calling a lower rate repayment.** An extended or modified loan can leave the count without being repaid.

## What we don't know

We have not compared the publishers' methodology notes line by line, so the three tests above are general and may not match any one publisher's rule. We do not know why the two Hilton figures differ, and we found no CRED iQ reading to cite. The rates will change with the next report. Read the figures here as examples of the gap, not a current reading.
