When a securitized hotel loan defaults, it moves to a [special servicer](/glossary/#special-servicer). The servicer acts for the investors in the securitization, under rules in the securitization documents, and its job is the best recovery for them. A [note sale](/glossary/#note-sale) is one of its tools. It is a sale of the loan, not the hotel: the owner keeps the hotel, and the loan documents stay as they are.

## How a servicer compares its options

A servicer does not simply pick the largest number. It estimates what each route is likely to bring in, subtracts the cost, such as legal fees, receiver costs, and [protective advances](/glossary/#protective-advance), and discounts the result for time and risk. That is a present-value comparison: a smaller sum now can be worth more than a larger sum later.

How much room the servicer has, and whom it must ask first, depends on the securitization documents. Each route changes the comparison differently:

- **[Modification](/glossary/#loan-modification).** Wins if the hotel's cash flow can carry the debt and the owner is capable. Loses if it only defers the loss while the building ages and the brand's patience wears.
- **[Discounted payoff](/glossary/#discounted-payoff).** Wins when someone has the money and the figure beats the alternatives. Loses if the funds are uncertain or the discount is deeper than a sale would give.
- **Foreclosure, then a sale of the property.** Can produce the highest figure on paper. It takes longest, and meanwhile the trust carries an operating hotel, with receiver costs, advances, and a franchise to protect.
- **Note sale.** Fixes the recovery on a date, with no legal budget, no advances, and no hotel to run. The price reflects the time and risk the buyer takes on, so it is lower than a successful workout might yield.

Delay costs a hotel more than most property, because its value rests on a franchise, staff, and bookings that do not wait. See [foreclosure timelines and why they matter more for hotels](/blog/foreclosure-timelines-and-hotel-value/).

**Illustration (invented numbers):** A servicer holds an $8 million loan and discounts cash at 10% a year. Foreclosure and a later sale might net $5.1 million in two years, worth $4.2 million today. A modification might return $5.0 million in three years, worth $3.8 million today. An owner can pay $4.0 million now. A note sale brings $4.5 million within months.

On those figures the note sale is worth the most today, though foreclosure shows the biggest number on paper. Change the sale price or the time foreclosure takes and the order changes, so a servicer reruns the comparison for each hotel.

## How a sale runs, and how bids are compared

Sales are often run through an advisor. Interested buyers sign a confidentiality agreement and receive information about the loan and the hotel. They complete their review and submit a bid by a set date. The servicer chooses a buyer and the parties sign a loan sale agreement.

Bids are compared on price, certainty, conditions, and timing. A higher bid with a financing contingency, a right to reopen the price, or a long closing can be worth less than a lower, clean one, because a failed sale costs months in which the hotel keeps losing value.

## Why sales fail

Sales fail for a few repeating reasons. A financing contingency lets the buyer walk if its own funding does not arrive. A bid made on a thin look can turn into a request for a cut once the buyer sees the whole file. The person who bid may lack the authority to close without approvals still to come.

Consent can also stall a deal. Where the documents give the [controlling class](/glossary/#controlling-class) a say, a sale the servicer has agreed may wait until that class consents. It usually sits lowest in the structure, so a discounted price may fall on it first.

## What a bidder is expected to bring

Servicers tend to ask for much the same things:

- **Diligence done before the bid.** Loan documents, servicing history, appraisal, franchise agreement and [comfort letter](/glossary/#comfort-letter), improvement plan, and operating results, with no expectation of reopening the price afterwards.
- **The seller's documents.** The agreement is the seller's form, usually as-is, with limited representations.
- **A deposit on signing, proof of funds, and no financing contingency.**
- **A certification of non-affiliation** with the borrower, guarantor, or sponsor.
- **Authority.** The person who submits the bid must be able to close it.
- **Closing on the seller's schedule.**
