A lender holding a defaulted hotel loan has three broad choices, and each has a cost that does not appear in the loan balance.

**Extending** keeps the customer and defers the loss, but the loan stays classified and continues to take up capital and staff attention. If the hotel does not recover, the lender faces the same decision later, with an older property and a less patient brand.

**Foreclosing** can produce the highest recovery on paper. It also takes the longest, especially where foreclosure runs through the courts, and it makes the lender the owner of an operating business it must staff, license, insure, and eventually sell.

**Selling the loan** fixes the recovery on a known date. The price reflects the time and risk the buyer takes on, so it is lower than a successful workout might yield. In exchange the lender is finished: no legal budget, no protective advances, no hotel to run.

The right answer depends on how much has already been reserved against the loan, how long enforcement takes in that state, and where the hotel is heading while the process runs.
