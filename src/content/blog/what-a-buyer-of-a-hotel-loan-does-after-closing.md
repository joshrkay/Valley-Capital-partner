A defaulted hotel loan can change hands in a day. What happens next takes longer, and it follows a fairly regular order. This describes common practice, not any one buyer's. Each step asks for something the owner can prepare.

## The first weeks

**Notice and payment instructions.** The owner is told who the new holder is and where payments go, and the [assignment of the mortgage](/glossary/#assignment-of-mortgage) is usually recorded. The buyer needs payments to reach the right account, so the owner checks the instructions through a contact already known.

**A review of the file.** The buyer had the file before it bid, and now checks the servicing history, earlier agreements, the guarantor, the franchise, and taxes and insurance against the real hotel. The owner helps with signed copies of every agreement and a record of each payment.

**A request for information.** Operating statements, performance reports, franchise status, and the [improvement plan](/glossary/#pip) and its deadline. Stale figures cannot support a decision, so current, dated numbers that match the manager's reports speed what follows.

**A conversation.** What the owner wants, what the hotel can carry, and what capital the owner can raise. Some holders first ask for a [prenegotiation letter](/glossary/#prenegotiation-letter), which lets both sides talk without giving up their rights. The owner's counsel should read it.

## The five usual endings

- **A [modification](/glossary/#loan-modification) or extension,** sometimes after a [forbearance](/glossary/#forbearance), fits when the hotel can carry its debt on reset terms and the owner is cooperative. It needs current numbers and conditions such as a fee or a paydown, and breaks when the numbers do not support the new terms.
- **A [discounted payoff](/glossary/#discounted-payoff)** fits when the owner can raise new capital or refinance part of the balance. It needs proof of the money and a release of the lien, and breaks when funds arrive late or other creditors, such as tax authorities, have claims too.
- **A negotiated transfer,** often by [deed in lieu of foreclosure](/glossary/#deed-in-lieu), fits when the owner prefers to exit and wants a release. It needs a hotel still operating, with franchise and license in place, and breaks when the brand refuses the new owner, a license cannot move in time, or unpaid taxes cloud title.
- **A sale by the owner** fits when a purchaser will pay a price the holder accepts as payoff. It breaks when the purchaser's franchise application, financing, or license transfer fails.
- **Enforcement.** [Foreclosure](/glossary/#foreclosure), usually with a [receiver](/glossary/#receivership) running the hotel, fits when no agreement can be reached. It needs funds for costs and advances. A bankruptcy filing can pause it, and a court's calendar can slow it.

Many buyers prefer an agreed ending, because enforcement is slower and costs more.

**Illustration (invented numbers):** A holder has a $10 million loan on a hotel now worth $8 million. A discounted payoff of $7 million arrives in month 3. A deed in lieu in month 4 is followed by $0.5 million of costs and a sale in month 10 at $7.8 million, netting $7.3 million. Enforcement takes 18 months, the hotel slips to $7 million, and $1 million of costs leave $6 million.

Enforcement looks strongest on paper, since it ends in title to a hotel worth $8 million today, yet here it is the slowest and nets the least, before counting the cost of waiting. Other facts could reorder the three.

## What drives speed

Cash flow decides most, because a hotel whose income does not cover its debt payments cannot carry a modification. The brand's cure periods and improvement deadlines set a clock, and the owner's capital, the guarantor's position, and [how long enforcement takes in that state](/blog/foreclosure-timelines-and-hotel-value/) also weigh in.

There is no fixed timeline, so the owner cannot plan around a date the buyer names. An agreed ending can take months, and enforcement longer. The dates that bind are in the documents: the maturity date, the end of a forbearance, and the brand's deadlines.

## Buyers differ

Buyers want different end results. Some prefer the loan to keep performing, so their interest runs with the hotel's recovery. Others expect to end up with the hotel, and weigh a modification against enforcement.

An owner is entitled to ask a buyer which it is, and to ask for the answer in writing. The answer is not a promise, because facts change, but it can be checked against later conduct. For the owner's side, see [what changes, and what does not, when your hotel loan is sold](/blog/when-your-hotel-loan-is-sold/).

## What we don't know

We have not seen a fixed order or timetable that every buyer follows, and we do not describe one. Sequence and timing depend on the loan documents, the hotel, and the state.
