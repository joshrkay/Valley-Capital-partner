When a securitized hotel loan defaults, it moves to a [special servicer](/glossary/#special-servicer). The servicer's job is to get the best recovery for the investors it works for. It has several tools: a [modification](/glossary/#loan-modification), a [discounted payoff](/glossary/#discounted-payoff), foreclosure followed by a sale of the property, or a [note sale](/glossary/#note-sale).

## Why a note sale

A note sale has two attractions for a servicer. It is faster than enforcement, and it fixes the recovery on a date. For a hotel, where delay erodes value, that can matter a great deal. It also moves the work of managing the borrower, the franchise, and any receivership to the buyer.

## How a sale usually runs

Sales are often run through an advisor. Interested buyers sign a confidentiality agreement and receive information about the loan and the hotel. They complete their review, then submit a bid by a set date. The servicer chooses a buyer and the parties sign a loan sale agreement.

## What a bidder is expected to bring

Servicers tend to ask for much the same things:

- **Diligence done before the bid.** Bidders review the loan documents, servicing history, appraisal, franchise agreement and [comfort letter](/glossary/#comfort-letter), improvement plan, and operating results before they bid, and do not expect to reopen the price afterwards.
- **The seller's documents.** The agreement is the seller's form, usually as-is, with limited representations.
- **A deposit on signing.**
- **Proof of funds, and no financing contingency.** A servicer wants to know the buyer can close.
- **A certification of non-affiliation.** The bidder confirms it has no affiliation with the borrower, guarantor, or sponsor.
- **Authority.** The person who submits the bid must be able to close it.
- **Closing on the seller's schedule.**

A bidder that meets these terms is easy to deal with, and a servicer can usually tell quickly whether a bidder will.
