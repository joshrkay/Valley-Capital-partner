# How people in the market talk about hotel loan distress

Research date: November 12, 2026. Window: roughly November 2025 to November 2026 (questions pass: November 2024 to November 2026). This is the companion to `docs/research/peer-voice.md`, which covers how firms *publish*. This one covers how practitioners *speak*, what owners, guarantors and would-be buyers *ask*, and which words they use.

## Bottom line

1. **Practitioners are blunt about specifics and measured about adjectives.** The candid lines are a number, a date or a condition ("hard money Day One"). The adjectives stay level. About ten examples, so low confidence.
2. **What sellers want from a buyer is certainty.** It is the one demand repeated across brokers, advisers and investors. No bank special-assets officer and no hotel owner was quoted saying what they want from a buyer, so this is the view from the intermediaries.
3. **The most common plain-language question is about the guarantor and about what a sale changes.** "Am I personally on the hook?" and "If my loan is sold, does the guaranty follow?" appear in first person. No plain answer was found for the second one.
4. **Owners and experts use different words for the same things.** "The bank sold my loan" is a note sale. "Hand back the keys" is a deed in lieu. "Non-recourse" is assumed to mean safe and usually has carve-outs. Starting from the reader's sentence is the gap to close.
5. **Nobody was found asking for louder commentary.** The sources that comment on tone lean calm: the maturity wall "sorts" hotels, distress is "asset-specific rather than systemic." "Vulture" appears only in a 2020 piece. This is thin evidence, not a test.
6. **The evidence is thinnest where we most need it:** no bank officer on selling hotel loans, and almost no public first-person hotel-owner voice.

## Method and limits (read this first)

Two passes, about 120 web searches in all, using search only. **Nothing was fetched or read in full.** Search returns condensed summaries of pages, so quoted wording may be the tool's paraphrase. Quotes must be checked against the original page before any external use. Four quotes the agent flagged as most uncertain (a Dekel Capital "extend and pretend" attribution, an MCA Financial case-study phrase, an HVS "BOV pipeline" line, and a "driver's seat" law-firm line) are left out below.

- **Refused or empty:** Reddit (the search tool refuses the domain), LinkedIn (only profiles and old general articles), Avvo and Justia (nothing), AAHOA member forums (nothing), Facebook groups (not searched).
- **Dates:** many forum threads are undated and several look older than the window. Dates marked "c." were inferred from search-result age.
- **Asker voice comes from adjacent venues:** BiggerPockets, JustAnswer and Quora, mostly residential or non-US. Carrying it over to hotels is inference.

The raw notes from both passes are in the session scratchpad, not in the repo.

### Vocabulary

- **Plain words in use:** patience, haircuts, "doesn't pencil", keys and "per key", "pennies on the dollar" (The Real Deal headline, April 2, 2026, https://therealdeal.com/san-francisco/2026/04/02/hyatt-house-hotels-in-east-bay-sell-at-foreclosure/), "danger zone" for debt yield below 9% (Lodging, August 27, 2026, https://lodgingmagazine.com/the-refinancing-wall-is-sorting-hotels-not-punishing-them/).
- **Trade jargon:** special servicing, forbearance, maturity default, debt yield, DSCR, BOV, PSA, PIP, key money, flag, comfort letter, credit bid, deed in lieu, note sale, loan auction, held for sale, stalking horse, going-concern value.
- **Metaphors:** "wall" (the maturity wall; Lodging calls it "sorting, not punishing"), "squeeze" (Skift, April 13, 2026), "holds which cards" (a lawyer's column, Commercial Observer, November 2026).
- **Contested terms:**
  - *Distressed or stressed.* HVS says hospitality "is no longer viewed as distressed" and that distress is "asset-specific rather than systemic" (February 2026, https://www.hvs.com/article/10378-mba-cref-2026-conference-key-takeaways-debt-markets-liquidity-and-the-evolving-real-estate-cycle).
  - *Delinquent, nonperforming or special-serviced.* Same brand, different measures: KBRA near 16% delinquent against Trepp's 11.73% nonperforming (see `peer-voice.md`).
  - *Receivership.* Owners' counsel call it value-destroying ("would severely impair going-concern value", a debtor's filing, March 8, 2026, https://elevenflo.com/cases/by-hotel-spe-3-llc-5364); lenders and receivers treat it as a tool.
  - *Loan-to-own.* Lenders disown it (a 2020 GlobeSt piece, outside the window); law firms present buying the debt as a route to the asset.
- **Stock phrase:** "extend and pretend" appears in a law-firm headline, Hospitality Investor and Bisnow. It implies lenders are fooling themselves.

### What people ask

Thirty-one questions were collected. The themes, with a representative case each:
1. **Refinancing at maturity:** "When do you begin the refi process for a commercial mortgage?" (BiggerPockets, undated, https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/49/topics/1180025-when-do-you-begin-the-refi-process-for-a-commercial-mortgage).
2. **Personal exposure:** "I signed a personal guarantee on a real estate loan (made to an LLC…" and "I AM 1 OF 5 PERSONAL GUARANTORS" (JustAnswer, undated, https://www.justanswer.com/law/7mrg2-signed-personal-guarantee-real-estate-loan-made.html).
3. **Does an LLC or "non-recourse" protect me:** carve-outs can make a non-recourse loan "suddenly" recourse (WealthManagement, undated, https://www.wealthmanagement.com/wealth-management-industry-trends/how-bad-boy-guarantees-can-make-a-non-recourse-loan-suddenly-become-recourse).
4. **If my loan is sold, does the guaranty follow:** a forum responder said the new holder can pursue all guarantors unless the sale says otherwise (BiggerPockets, undated; attribution to this thread is likely, not confirmed).
5. **Can the bank sell my loan without my consent:** forum posters say yes (BiggerPockets, about 2014); a federal consumer FAQ says no for residential loans (https://bankcustomer.gov/get-answers/mortgages/general-mortgage-questions/faq-mortgage-general-03.html). No commercial-loan source was found.
6. **Will I lose my flag:** law-firm material says to check the comfort letter during a default (Goulston & Storrs, undated, https://www.goulstonstorrs.com/content/uploads/pdf/publication/franchise-agreements-and-loans-the-comfort-letter-and-beyond-2.pdf). Nothing found on a note sale specifically.
7. **What does a note buyer do after closing:** forum consensus is that foreclosure is the baseline plan, modifications often re-default, and many buyers prefer keeping the borrower in place (BiggerPockets, undated, https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/70/topics/298880-how-do-i-get-started-investing-in-non-performing-notes). Residential, not hotel.
8. **How to price a note:** residential posters cite roughly 30 to 50% of collateral value, rising to 50 to 70% (https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/70/topics/251608-npn-market-pricing-feedback). Hotel pieces cite debt yield and PIP cost. No hotel pricing formula was found.

### Misconceptions, fears and distrust

- **"Non-recourse means no personal exposure,"** and **"an LLC shields me."** Both are contradicted in the sources above.
- **"Buying the note means getting the hotel."** CoStar says buyers treat it that way, and a bankruptcy can leave them a long-term lender (about 2010).
- **Distrust of special servicers** shows up in trade press and a union advisory: borrower experience "still hell" (GlobeSt, December 3, 2019, https://globest.com/2019/12/03/the-cmbs-borrower-experience-is-still-hell), accusations of collecting fees while loans linger (Law360, June 25, 2024).
- **Distrust of loan buyers is thin.** One borrower alleges a note buyer rejected a payoff the prior lender had agreed (Pincus & Co., undated, an allegation only, https://www.pincusco.com/moin-development-says-billionaire-family-office-blocking-100m-hotel-refi/). On forums, buyers say they want the borrower to keep paying.
- **Contradictions to avoid repeating:** whether a bank needs consent to sell a loan; whether lenders are still extending; whether a receiver acts for the owner (a UK source) or displaces the owner (US hotel material).

## Inferences

- Hotel owners probably ask these questions privately, to lawyers, brokers and peers, which would explain the thin public record.
- Note-sale language lives in brokers' and reporters' mouths, not in bank officers'.
- Honesty about condition (deferred renovations, PIP cost) is implied in broker and lender talk but nobody states it as a want.

## Recommendations

**Voice** (applied to `docs/voice.md`):
- Prefer words already in use: maturity, extension, haircut, per key, flag, PIP, debt yield, special servicing. For a hotel say "stressed" or "asset-specific"; keep "non-performing" for a loan, as its documents and the cited source define it.
- Start from the reader's sentence ("the bank sold my loan"), then give the term and link the glossary.
- Use "extend and pretend" only as a quoted market phrase. It tells lenders they are fooling themselves.
- Promise "certainty of execution", "proof of funds" or a "hard deposit" only if the firm will deliver them.
- Say "it depends on your documents and your state" once per post, not in every sentence.

**Topics for sale posts** (each of these has no good plain answer found): what changes for the guarantor; what changes for the franchise; what a buyer does after closing, in order and without a fixed timeline; what a borrower can ask a buyer to confirm in writing (who holds the loan now, who services it, the payoff figure); how pricing works.

**Glossary candidates** (terms in use that the glossary lacks): non-recourse carve-out ("bad boy" guaranty), debt yield, broker opinion of value (BOV), credit bid, haircut, per key, prenegotiation letter, master servicer, controlling holder, proof of funds.
