The franchise agreement is between the brand and the hotel's owner. The lender is not a party to it. A lender's rights with the brand come from a separate comfort letter, signed by the brand, the owner, and the lender.

A comfort letter usually gives the lender notice of a franchise default, time to cure it, and a path to a new franchise if the lender takes title. Whether those rights pass to a buyer of the loan depends on the letter. Many require the brand's consent to an assignment, or a replacement letter issued to the new holder.

Before bidding, we read the letter for its assignment terms. With the seller's consent, we ask the brand to confirm the franchise's standing, any unpaid fees, and the status of required improvements. We plan for a new franchise application if the hotel changes hands.
