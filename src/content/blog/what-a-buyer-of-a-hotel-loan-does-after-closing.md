A defaulted hotel loan can change hands in a day. What happens next takes longer, and it follows a fairly regular order. This describes common practice, not any one buyer's.

## The first weeks

1. **Notice and payment instructions.** The owner is told who the new holder is and where payments go. The [assignment of the mortgage](/glossary/#assignment-of-mortgage) is usually recorded in the land records.
2. **A review of the file.** The buyer had the loan file before it bid. After closing it goes back through the servicing history, any earlier agreements with the owner, the status of the guarantor, the franchise, and taxes and insurance.
3. **A request for information.** Operating statements, performance reports, the franchise status, and the [improvement plan](/glossary/#pip) and its deadline.
4. **A conversation.** What the owner wants, what the hotel can carry, and what capital the owner can raise.

## The five usual endings

- **A modification or extension,** if the hotel can carry its debt. See [loan modification](/glossary/#loan-modification) and [forbearance](/glossary/#forbearance).
- **A discounted payoff,** funded by the owner or by outside capital. See [discounted payoff](/glossary/#discounted-payoff).
- **A negotiated transfer of the hotel,** often by [deed in lieu of foreclosure](/glossary/#deed-in-lieu), sometimes with a release of the guaranty.
- **A sale of the hotel by the owner,** with the loan repaid from the proceeds.
- **Enforcement.** [Foreclosure](/glossary/#foreclosure), usually with a [receiver](/glossary/#receivership) running the hotel in the meantime.

Many buyers prefer an agreed ending, because enforcement is slower and costs more. Where no agreement can be reached they rely on the rights in the loan documents.

## What decides which one

- Whether the hotel's cash flow covers its debt payments.
- What the brand requires, what it costs, and how patient the brand is.
- The capital the owner has, or can find.
- The position of the guarantor.
- How long enforcement takes in that state. See [foreclosure timelines and why they matter more for hotels](/blog/foreclosure-timelines-and-hotel-value/).

## How long it takes

There is no fixed timeline. An agreed ending can take months. Enforcement can take longer, especially where foreclosure runs through the courts.

## Buyers differ

Buyers differ in what they want the end result to be. Some prefer the loan to keep performing. Others expect to end up with the hotel. An owner is entitled to ask a buyer which it is, and to ask for the answer in writing. For the owner's side of this, see [what changes, and what does not, when your hotel loan is sold](/blog/when-your-hotel-loan-is-sold/).

## What we don't know

We have not seen a fixed order or timetable that every buyer follows, and we do not describe one here. Sequence and timing depend on the loan documents, the hotel, and the state.
