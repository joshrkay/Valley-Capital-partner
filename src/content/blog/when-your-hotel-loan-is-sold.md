Hotel owners are sometimes told that their lender has sold their loan. It can be unsettling, but the position is simpler than it feels. A [note sale](/glossary/#note-sale) changes who holds the loan. It does not change the loan.

## What stays the same

The note, the mortgage, and any [guaranty](/glossary/#guaranty) stay as they are. The obligations and rights in those documents pass to the new holder unchanged, including the maturity date and the terms of any [forbearance](/glossary/#forbearance) or [modification](/glossary/#loan-modification) already in place.

## What changes

The person making decisions. A new holder that bought the loan at a different price may be willing to agree to terms the original lender could not. It may also have a different view of the hotel, the franchise, and the timetable. Neither is guaranteed, which is why it is worth finding out early.

## What to expect

- **A notice of transfer.** It should say who the new holder is and where payments are to go.
- **A request for information.** Operating statements, performance reports, and the status of the franchise and any [improvement plan](/glossary/#pip) are typical.
- **A proposal.** That might be a modification, a payoff figure, or a negotiated transfer of the hotel.

## What an owner can do

1. Read the notice carefully, and confirm the new payment instructions in writing before changing anything. Do not keep paying the old lender, and do not stop paying.
2. Gather current operating statements, the franchise status, and a clear picture of any renovation requirements.
3. Be ready to explain what you want: to keep the hotel, to pay off at a discount, or to exit.
4. Speak to your own counsel before negotiating, especially about any guaranty.

Most owners and holders prefer an agreed outcome, because it is faster and cheaper for both. Where agreement cannot be reached, the holder can rely on the rights in the loan documents, which is why the same preparation matters either way.

This is general information, not legal advice.
